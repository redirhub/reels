# Script craft notes — what high-retention explainers actually do

One page, written before script v3 and applied to it. Research on 2026-10-06 across Kurzgesagt,
Vox, Fireship, Johnny Harris, Ali Abdaal, MKBHD and Apple's product films (transcripts, interviews
and published breakdowns; sources at the end). We are not imitating any of them. We are taking the
craft and building our own voice: **a smart, transparent friend explaining something over coffee.**

## The first 15–30 seconds

- **The first sentence is the thesis or the puzzle. Never a greeting.** MKBHD speaks his verdict
  2.4 s in, before the logo ("So, this is peak slab phone."). Fireship defines the thing in line
  one. Kurzgesagt opens on a concrete, present-tense image ("Right now, 100 million radiation-blasted
  flies are raining down over the jungles of Panama").
- **Make the viewer feel the problem before showing the fix.** Vox's Joss Fong refused to open the
  black-hole video with the photo: "I wanted to get people to understand why this is so difficult
  before they saw the result." Our hook list of concrete breakages is this move.
- **Promise one specific thing, then pay it off on screen.** Harris calls it the "promise"; Abdaal
  scripts only the hook line by line ("If you can lock in for 15 minutes…"). Ours: "the right way,
  and keep it secure. It's simpler than you think."

## Open loops and payoffs

- Curiosity is an **information gap** (Loewenstein): open one in the title and first line, open a
  smaller one every 10–15 s, and **close most of them inside the video** so the viewer trusts the
  next one. Abdaal's trick: an unfinished on-screen list that completes as the video goes.
- Our loops: *"It looks done"* (closed at the callback: *"Now it is."*), *"three ideas, then we do
  it"* (the recap row completes), the **twist** (*"it's your test that's wrong"*, closed 15 s later).

## "But / therefore", never "and then"

- Parker & Stone (NYU): "If the words *and then* belong between those beats, you're fucked. What
  should happen between every beat is either *therefore* or *but*." That is causation, and
  causation is a story. Every scene break in v3 is written as a *but* or a *therefore*.

## Specificity over abstraction

- "100 million flies over Panama", "1,200 hours", the exact `git init` command. Not "huge", not
  "a modern tool". For us: "the QR code on your menu", "the email from last year", "an A record and
  a TXT record", "mydomain.com → mybrand.com". Never "something quietly goes wrong".

## Pattern interrupts (without gimmicks)

- Change *something* every 20–40 s: Vox swaps the music track about every 20 s, MKBHD alternates
  face and product B-roll, Fireship drops one dry clause per sentence. The interrupt is a change in
  rhythm or register, not a visual trick. Ours: tip cards (register change), the rewind (time
  change), the product UI appearing (world change), silence before the end card.
- **Humour is a clause, not a sketch.** Fireship: merge conflicts → "you'll just have to wait for
  the sequel." One wry beat, then back to work. Ours: "Thirty seconds now, zero regret later."

## Sounding like a person, not a company

- **Second person, present tense, contractions.** "You just created a repo" (Fireship). Sentences
  of 6–14 words; one idea each; start some with "So" and "But" the way people do.
- **Signpost out loud.** Fong: listeners can't re-read, so say "That's right", "Here's the catch",
  "Step two". Hand-holding is kindness, not condescension.
- **Say what it doesn't do and show the seams.** Kurzgesagt publishes 15–50 page source sheets and
  made a video about their own mistakes; MKBHD: "If the product is bad, that's my job to share
  that." Transparency *is* the voice. Ours: "we make RedirHub, so that's what I'll use", "some
  registrars do this for you, some don't, here's how to check", "copy yours, not mine".
- **Script every word, perform like you didn't.** MKBHD is 98 % scripted "from the beginning to
  thanks for watching" and sounds off the cuff. Density comes from writing; warmth from delivery.
- **Avoid Apple-grade abstraction.** "This is what matters" tells; Slate called the 2013 campaign
  "underlying arrogance… it tells, it doesn't show." We can borrow Apple's *quiet* (one object,
  one idea, silence) but a friend shows the screen.

## Rules applied to script v3 (checklist)

1. First line is the thesis, no greeting. 2. Problem felt before the fix (five concrete breakages,
one per screen). 3. One promise, paid off on screen (three green checks). 4. Every beat joined by
*but* or *therefore*. 5. One idea per 8–14 s beat. 6. A loop every ~15 s; all closed by the end.
7. Concrete nouns, no adjectives doing the work. 8. "You", present tense, contractions. 9. Spoken
signposts ("Idea one", "Step two", "Here's the catch"). 10. Register change every 20–40 s.
11. Humour as a clause. 12. Seams shown (disclosure, fairness, "copy yours"). 13. Every word
scripted; read aloud for rhythm. 14. Quiet, not abstract.
