## ACT A — Meet Frosty Fusion

`[w44-Shop. Cold open on the light room; the Frosty Fusion homepage is already there.]`

**2.** Five years of scooping. Forty flavors. And one strawberry that people drive across town for.
`[Three number stickers pop on her three numbers; the strawberry tile lifts and takes its ring on "strawberry".]`

**3.** *([curious])* Now, type "best strawberry ice cream" into Google, anywhere near their shop…
`[w44-Search. The scoop icon flies into the search card; the query writes itself. "Example search".]`

**4.** *([proud])* …and there they are. Right at the top.
`[The strawberry card rises into slot 1.]`

**5.** That spot didn't happen by luck. It's SEO.
`[w44-SEO. Room change to the Glow. The headline lands on "SEO"; the address pill sits in the middle.]`

**6.** SEO, search engine optimization, is everything that helps Google decide your page is the best answer to a search.
`["What makes Google pick your page." under the headline.]`

**7.** For Frosty Fusion, that's five years of it. Pages Google trusts. Food blogs that linked to their strawberry. Reviews that mention them by name.
`[A rope draws from the pill to each card as she names it: pages, a food blog, reviews.]`

**8.** *([pause])* And all of it is tied to one address: getfrostyfusion.com.
`["All tied to one address." The pill brightens; everything holds.]`

`[w44-Smoothie. Back to the light room; the Frosty Fusion sign hangs.]`

**11.** One ordered a green detox.
`[Bubble two pops: "One green detox, please."]`

**12.** *([sighs])* Fair. "Fusion" does sound a little… blended.
`[A striped straw drops into the swirl. "Fair. 'Fusion' does sound a little… blended."]`

**13.** *([excited])* So, a new name. Frosty Bites. Short, sweet, and nobody's ordering kale. New domain: getfrostybites.com.
`[w44-Rename. The swirl splits into two scoops, the blue one smiles, "Fusion" rolls to "Bites". BEFORE / NOW. The pill rolls to getfrostybites.com.]`

**14.** *([pause])* And then comes the thought that keeps every business owner up at night.
`[w44-Worry. Room change to the Glow.]`

**15.** *([worried])* What happens to the Google spot? The strawberry page. The five years.
`[The strawberry card slides back in with a question mark.]`

**16.** Change the domain the wrong way, and Google meets Frosty Bites like a total stranger.
`[The new site arrives alone, with three empty sockets: no links, no reviews, no history.]`

**17.** *([reassuring])* Do it right, and Google can carry your rankings over to the new name. You don't start from zero.
`[The sockets glow teal, one by one.]`

**18.** So how do you change your domain name without losing SEO? It comes down to three steps.
`[w44-Title. Transition 1 of 2. The two pills with "becomes"; the title; "Three steps."]`

## ACT C — How Google sees your site

**19.** First, the thing most people get wrong. Google doesn't rank your domain. It ranks your pages.
`[w44-Pages. Charcoal. "Google ranks pages, not domains."]`

**20.** To Google, Frosty Fusion is a list of addresses. The homepage. The flavor menu. And that famous page: getfrostyfusion.com/flavors/strawberry.
`[The tree draws out from "/" as she names each page; the strawberry page last.]`

**21.** Each page has its own history. How long it has ranked, and who links to it.
`[A history badge pops on each page. "Example numbers".]`

**22.** Change the domain, and every one of those addresses changes too. The new ones start with no history.
`[w44-Flip. The domain on each card rolls to the new one; each badge drops off on a red broken line. Room warms to the Glow.]`

**23.** So the job isn't moving a domain. It's moving every page, in a way Google can follow.
`["The history doesn't follow on its own."]`

**24.** Three steps. Map it. Redirect it. Tell Google.
`[w44-Steps. Brand radial. The three cards rise on her three words.]`

## ACT D — The three steps

**25.** Step one: the map.
`[w44-Map. Card 01 opens into the paper sheet. Progress 1 of 3.]`

**26.** Old address on the left, new address on the right. getfrostyfusion.com/flavors/strawberry becomes getfrostybites.com/flavors/strawberry.
`[Rows write in under the two domain headers; the strawberry row lifts.]`

**27.** If the paths stay the same, one rule covers every page. If some pages changed, each one gets its own line.
`[The teal bracket draws on "one rule"; the CHANGED tag pops on "its own line".]`

**28.** Quick tip: before you change anything, export your page list from your sitemap or from Google Search Console. That's your inventory.
`[The tip card slides in: "Export your page list first."]`

**29.** *([conspiratorial])* Now, here's where most domain changes quietly go wrong.
`[w44-Shortcut. The old page cards on the left.]`

**30.** Mapping every page feels like a chore. So people take a shortcut: send everything to the new homepage.
`[Every line bends into one homepage.]`

**31.** *([light])* And it looks perfect. Every old link lands somewhere. No errors. Strawberry fans arrive at getfrostybites.com.
`[Teal ticks on "No errors".]`

**32.** *([pause])* But they wanted the strawberry. Now they're on a homepage, hunting for it.
`[The cursor wanders the homepage: "Where's the strawberry?"]`

**33.** *([serious])* And Google notices too. Hundreds of pages, all poured into one. It can treat them as pages that no longer exist.
`[w44-Melt. THE CATCH. The Glow. The old pages strike through: "treated as gone".]`

**34.** *([softly])* And that top spot for "best strawberry ice cream"? It starts to melt.
`[The card drips and slides from #1 to #4, upright; one amber ring. Then one second of silence.]`

**35.** *([firm])* One old page, one matching new page. That's the rule.
`[w44-OneToOne. The film's only time-reverse: the drips run back up; each old page snaps to its twin with a 301 tag.]`

**36.** Step two: the redirects. Here's what that looks like in RedirHub.
`[w44-Migration. Transition 2 of 2 into the product. Progress 2 of 3.]`

**37.** Links. Create. Website Migration.
`[Three quick cuts on her three words: Links, the create chooser, Website Migration.]`

**38.** Old domain: getfrostyfusion.com. New domain: getfrostybites.com.
`[Old domain and New base URL fill as she says them.]`

**39.** Same paths? One same-path rule, and every page forwards to its twin.
`["Keep the same paths" rings; What happens fills; the strawberry row rings.]`

**40.** Changed pages? Paste your map, or upload it as a spreadsheet file, a CSV.
`[w44-Preview. "Map URLs one by one"; the rows paste in; the CSV card rings.]`

**41.** Before anything goes live, RedirHub shows you what it will create, and flags every row with a problem. Nothing is created until you click Create.
`[What happens fills; the skipped row takes a red ring; "Create 2 redirects".]`

**42.** Every redirect should be a 301, the permanent kind. It's the signal Google uses to carry a page's history to its new address.
`[Redirect type rings teal: 301 · Permanent.]`

**43.** Then point getfrostyfusion.com at RedirHub with the records it gives you. The padlock is set up automatically. Our earlier videos walk through it.
`[The DNS-and-padlock tip slides in.]`

**44.** Step three: tell Google you've moved.
`[w44-TellGoogle. Progress 3 of 3.]`

**45.** Google Search Console is Google's free dashboard for your site. Open the old site there, and use Change of Address to point it at the new one.
`[The generic console: the new site is picked; "Move requested" ticks.]`

**46.** Then submit the new sitemap: the list of pages you want Google to find.
`[The sitemap row ticks "Submitted".]`

**47.** And update everything you control. Your site's own links. Your Instagram bio. The QR code on the menu. Your Google Maps listing. All of it says Frosty Bites now.
`[w44-Everything. Each prop flips to Frosty Bites as she names it and takes a teal check.]`
