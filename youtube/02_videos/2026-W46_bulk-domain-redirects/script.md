## A · Your company  ·  current video 0:00–0:31
1. [warm] Imagine your company. Let's call it Northway, a small software company.
2. Every product it launched started the same way: buy the domain first. Then one for each country. A few misspellings, so nobody else grabs your visitors. And the old name, from before the rebrand.
3. [thoughtful] Today, Northway owns over two hundred domains. Whatever your business is, your list probably grew the same way.
4. [matter-of-fact] Now you're bringing all your products together on one website, so customers find everything in one place. That means every one of those two hundred domains needs a new destination.
5. [thoughtful] But the real question is, how do you move hundreds of domains at once, with one spreadsheet, without losing the people who use them?

## B · The quiet pile  ·  current video 0:31–0:54
6. Each domain renews every year, whether anyone uses it or not.
7. Most of them forward somewhere. Set up years ago, in whichever registrar account they were bought in. For Northway, that's three accounts.
8. Nobody has the full list. And nobody knows which ones people still type, like the name of a product you retired years ago.

## C · The visitor nobody sees  ·  current video 0:54–1:23
9. One evening, a customer types that old product domain. It forwards to Northway's site. Someone checked that once, years ago. It worked.
10. [curious] But the customer's browser asks for the secure version first, the one with the padlock. Some registrars' forwarding includes one. Others don't.
11. [quiet] So the customer sees a warning page. To them, it looks like Northway is broken. They leave.
12. Northway never finds out. Their own check worked only because they typed the plain address.

## D · The way out  ·  current video 1:23–1:50
13. [thoughtful] The fix wasn't fewer domains. Every domain needed two things: a door that opens properly, padlock included, and a way to hear when someone knocks.
14. One honest note. If you have a handful of domains, and your registrar's forwarding shows the padlock, you're done. Check one in our free Redirect Checker.
15. But two hundred domains, in three accounts, one form at a time? That's how domains get missed. So instead, you write down once where every domain should go. In a spreadsheet.

## E · One spreadsheet  ·  current video 1:50–3:00
16. Each row is one domain. Next to it, where it should go. The old product name to its new page. Each country to its own pages. The misspellings to the homepage.
17. Then the kind of redirect. A 301, the permanent kind, because these moves are for good.
18. And a tag, a short label for the group. Product. Country. Typo. It pays off later.
19. [warm] That one file is the whole plan, and anyone on the team can check it.
20. In RedirHub, it goes in through Links, Create, Import CSV. That's the plain spreadsheet file Excel or Google Sheets can save.
21. Before anything is saved, it shows what will happen. Every domain it adds, and any row that needs a second look.
22. A destination missing its https? One click fixes all of them. And each domain's www version comes along too.
23. One thing a spreadsheet can't do is tell the internet where each domain lives. That's DNS, the setting that points a domain somewhere.
24. On Cloudflare, it can connect automatically. Elsewhere, you add the records it shows you. In batches, if you like.
25. And as each domain connects, its padlock is set up, and renewed for it.

## F · Hearing the knock  ·  current video 3:00–3:33
26. [warm] Then, for the first time, Northway could hear the knocks. Visits, domain by domain.
27. A few weeks in, one stood out. A misspelling. Northway without the h. Sixty-three visits already.
28. [lightly] It had nearly been dropped at the last renewal.
29. [thoughtful] Those were customers, typing the name a little wrong. Now they land on the homepage, padlock and all.
30. The domains that stayed quiet all season? At renewal time, Northway knows where to look first.
31. And the tags pay off. Retiring a product? Filter by its tag. Change it once.
